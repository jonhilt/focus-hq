Bridge grammar Focus · how the captain works with goals, leads and the chief of staff · brownfield · draft 2, 28 Oct 2026

The job: steer several goals at once, across projects, without the work steering you. Today every goal arrives at the same volume, so you answer whichever spoke last and hop between them, and decisions reach you as text to read and a letter to pick. The main idea has two halves. One goal at the conn: you take the conn of one goal at a time and give it a watch. The others stay underway inside the orders you've already given, and what they need is mustered for when you stand down, unless it really can't wait. And judge the terrain, not the map: anything you'd decide by looking reaches you as an exhibit you can handle, and anything you'd decide by how it behaves gets probes, approaches built for real side by side. What you make so becomes an order on the goal's chart. Agent labour is cheap; your attention isn't. The lead does the translating.

Operational principles

Goal. You raise the goals that matter most to the top of the rail. You take the conn of one; the others stay underway, and the chief of staff keeps them going inside their orders. A goal awaiting someone outside the ship drops out of your way until they answer.
Watch. Taking the conn starts a watch. The goal fills the viewer, opening on what changed since your last watch on it, not on its whole history. When you stand down, the goal shrinks back into its berth and the muster comes up.
Decision. When a lead needs you, the chief of staff grades it. At the conn, it's in front of you. Underway, it adds one to that goal's count and waits for the muster. Only a hail breaks the watch, and you can put it on screen and tell it to stand by without leaving.
Exhibit. If you'd decide it by looking, the lead builds it to be handled. You answer its questions, see both sizes, mark what's wrong, open a channel to whoever else must agree, and make it so. Your answers stay on it, so nothing is picked twice.
Probe. When one part of a decision turns on how something will behave, the lead names the ways and you launch probes. Each is built for real on a branch against one real case, and they come back as a comparison. You try the case that worries you in all of them at once, make one so, and the rest are scuttled.
Muster. It gathers quietly during a watch, as a count. At watch change it comes up ordered by what each item unblocks. You clear it top to bottom, and reports wait folded at the end.

The stack

A thing sits as high as the attention it's asking of you right now. The higher it sits, the sooner it has to leave.

A hail asks the most, so it sits on top and is gone within a glance. The goal at the conn asks for your watch, so it's a sheet, and it stays up while you work. Goals underway ask nothing, so they rest low on the rail.

Anything that asks nothing of you never rises. Reports stay in the muster, and the chief of staff's words never become toasts.

Hull

The ground. In 3D it's the room you're standing in. In the panels it's the darkest surface, and it holds nothing of its own.

Rail

A berth for each goal, resting on the hull down the left edge, in the order you've set. Only the berth at the conn is raised.

Viewer

The sheet: the goal at the conn, or the muster when you stand down. Only one sheet is ever up. It grows out of its berth and shrinks back into it.

Card

Decisions, exhibits and probes rest on the sheet. Opening an exhibit lifts it to fill the sheet's body. The chart folds away, and the orders slide up to stay in view.

Panel

The comms panel slides in from the right edge. It pushes the sheet aside rather than covering it, so the conversation sits beside what it's about.

Hail

A popover. It drops from the Muster chip at the top right and goes back into it. It lives the shortest time of anything here.
