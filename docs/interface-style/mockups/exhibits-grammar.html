Exhibits grammar Focus · what a lead shows the captain on the bridge, and how none of it gets lost · brownfield · draft 2, 28 Oct 2026 · extends the bridge grammar

The job: see everything a lead puts in front of you, at your pace, and know which of it you've dealt with. Today an exhibit can vanish before you've looked at it: the sheet shows only the latest, showing it again overwrites it, and a new question takes the old one's place. The main idea: an exhibit stays on the sheet until you've had it. Exhibits arrive as small cards in a tray, in the order they came. You open one in place when you want it, and it shrinks back when you're done. Showing one again adds a version beside it and never swaps it under you. Got it files it on the waypoint of the slice it belongs to, where you can take it out again.

Shown again, overwrittenshow with the same id replaces the page: the version you hadn't seen yet, or had and want to compare against, is gone.
Shown another, out of sightThe sheet shows only the latest exhibit (latestArtifact), so the one before it drops out of view whether you'd read it or not.
Asked again, replacedask again replaces a question you haven't answered. And Focus keeps no record of which exhibits you've looked at.

Assumptions

  • This sits on the bridge as built (#716): the goal at the conn is the sheet, with its head, chart, orders and cards. Exhibits are one kind of card on it.
  • A lead shows a handful of exhibits per slice, not dozens. The tray holds what you haven't had, so it stays short if you keep up.
  • Looking is the point of an exhibit, so opening one counts as having seen it. Getting it is a separate, deliberate act: it means you're done with it.
  • Content below is a stand-in: Harbour & Lane is the client, the signing redesign the goal.

To confirm

  • Should exhibits you haven't had count on the goal's berth (a ring, not amber), or only in the since line?
  • Does the lead need a way to withdraw an exhibit ("ignore this one, it was wrong"), or is a new version enough?
  • When the tray holds more than fits (say 5), fold the oldest into "+3 more", or wrap to a second row?
  • Should a question asked again keep the earlier one as its version 1 (as drawn), or as a separate exhibit?

Operational principles

Exhibit. The lead shows something, and it lands at the end of the tray as a small card with a dot. You open it in place when you're ready; the dot becomes a ring. When you're done, Got it files it on its waypoint and the tray closes up.
Version. The lead shows the same exhibit again. If you're reading it, a line says v2 has arrived and what changed, and what you're reading stays put. You show v2 when you choose, compare it with v1 if you like, and Got it files both.
Question. The lead asks you to pick by looking. It's a decision card on the sheet; each option opens in place. Asked again, the new question carries the old one as its first version.
Waypoint's shelf. Exhibits you've had sit on the waypoint of their slice, counted. Press the count and they list from it; take one out and it goes back on the tray, seen.

The stack

An exhibit stays on the sheet until you've had it.

The lead only ever adds. A new exhibit goes on the end of the tray, a new version beside the one you're reading. Nothing that arrives takes the place of something already there.

Size is yours to choose. Exhibits rest small, and one at a time grows to full size in place. Only Got it moves one off the sheet, and it goes down onto the chart rather than away.

Card, open in place

The same card grown to the tray's width. Its neighbours step aside into a column beside it. At most one is open.

Popover

A waypoint's shelf, listed from the waypoint. It's gone once you take something out or look away.

1 · One arrives while you read another

Today: you're reading the data flow when the lead shows the layout options. The sheet now shows the layout options, and the data flow is gone from view.

The data flow rests in the tray as a small card with a dot. Opening it grows the card in place to the tray's width, and the dot becomes a ring.

The lead shows the layout options. A second small card slides onto the end of the tray, into the column beside the open exhibit, with a dot. The since line counts one new exhibit. The data flow doesn't move.

Got it on the data flow shrinks it down into the Signing flow waypoint, whose shelf goes from 1 to 2. The layout options card slides back into first place. Opening it grows it in place.

Why. What you're reading is yours until you put it down. A dot asks you to look; it doesn't make you look now. You can lose your place, but never an exhibit.

2 · The same exhibit, shown again

What's on a slice

In draft 1, a waypoint's shelf holds the exhibits you've had whose slice is that one, where "slice" is the slice the exhibit was last shown in (GoalArtifact.slice). So it isn't everything shown during the slice: an exhibit reworked later moves to the later slice, and one you haven't had isn't on any shelf.

A cleaner rule, used in every alternative below: each version belongs to the slice it was shown in. A slice then holds everything put in front of you during it (its exhibits by version, had or not), beside what it shipped (its pull requests) and what you decided (its captain-decision lines, by time). An exhibit reworked across slices appears in both, v1 in the first and v2 in the second. The tray still holds only what you haven't had.

A is draft 1 (the scenarios on the Layers tab). B to E each keep the tray and Got it, and change where progress sits and how you reach an earlier slice's exhibits. The same goal in all of them: Signing redesign, three slices in, Portal next.

B · The log down the side

The sheet's left column is the goal's log, one row per slice: next at the top, dashed, then where you are, then what's landed, newest first. The current slice is a raised row with its exhibits as thumbnails, what's open and what waits on you. Earlier slices are flat rows with tiny thumbnails and a line of what they shipped.

Pressing an earlier row opens it in place in the log, its exhibits full-size thumbnails you can open on the sheet. The sheet's right side keeps the decision and the tray.

GoodReads like a ship's log. Room for each slice's name, its PRs and decisions as well as its exhibits. Scales to many slices by scrolling.
CostsTakes a column from the sheet. Progress is a list, so "how far along" is less of a glance than a line.

D · The tray is the timeline

The chart and the tray merge into one strip. Each slice is a section of it, under its name and a pip on one track. Earlier slices are stacks of their exhibits, fanned, with a count. The current slice's exhibits lie out full, and the next slice is a dashed empty slot.

Pressing a stack fans it out in place; pressing a card opens it on the sheet. The decision sits under the strip.

GoodProgress and exhibits are one thing, so there's one place to look. The present gets the room and the past stays a press away. Least chrome of all.
CostsA slice with no exhibits is a thin section with just its name. PRs and decisions aren't on the strip; they'd sit in the fanned stack's footer.

Draft pick: D. It answers both asks at once (where are we, and where's that thing I saw) without a second structure.
