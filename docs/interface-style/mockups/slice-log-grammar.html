Slice log grammar Focus · the goal at the conn: what's asking you, and where everything lives · brownfield · draft 1, 28 Oct 2026 · alternative B, from first principles

The job: at the conn of one goal, see at once what it's asking of you, deal with it in order, and trust that nothing is lost when you do. The main idea: everything on a goal has a home in its slice. The sheet is two things side by side. On the left the log, every slice as a row, newest at the top: the goal's memory, where things rest when they ask nothing. On the right the desk: whatever is asking you now, as high as it asks. A decision is raised, with amber. An exhibit to look at rests, with a dot. Reports are a line. When you answer, the thing travels back into its slice's row and becomes a line of its record, so you see it filed.

Everything at one heightOn today's sheet the decision, the exhibit, the chart, the orders and the since line sit side by side, so the eye has to sort what's asking from what's history.
History is namesThe chart strip says a slice existed. What it shipped, what you decided in it and what you saw are in the ledger, not on the screen.
A slice ends, its asks driftWhen a slice lands, nothing ties a question still open to the slice that raised it; it reads as if it came from the new one.

Assumptions

  • This is the sheet of the goal at the conn on the bridge (#716). The rail, the muster and hails are unchanged.
  • A slice's record can be built from what Focus already keeps: waypoints, the lead's pull requests by slice, captain-decision lines by time, exhibits by the slice each version was shown in.
  • Asks on one goal are few at a time (a decision or two, a few exhibits), so the desk rarely scrolls.
  • Harbour & Lane and the signing redesign are stand-ins.

To confirm

  • Should a decision you open but don't answer stay raised, or step down to resting until it changes?
  • When a slice lands, should its unanswered asks follow you to the new slice's row, or stay homed to the old one (as drawn)?
  • How much record should the current slice's row show without opening it: the latest three lines (as drawn), or only counts?
  • Do reports need the desk at all, or could they go straight into the record with a quiet count?

Operational principles

Desk. You take the conn and look right: the highest thing on the desk is what to do first. You answer it, it travels into its slice's row, and the next thing rises. When the desk is calm, the goal is getting on without you.
Decision. A lead asks; a raised card lands on the desk's stack, ordered by what it unblocks, its home slice's row counting it in amber. You make it so; it files into that row as "Decided · …".
Slice. Its row says what it's asking and its latest record. Expand an earlier one and it opens in place in the log, its whole record; take out anything in it and it rises to the desk again.
Report. What the lead's crew want you to know lands on one line at the foot of the desk. Got it hears them all, and they file as one record line.

The stack

Everything has a home in its slice. It rises out of it while it asks for you, and settles back when you've answered.

Height on the desk is how much it asks: deciding is highest and stacked, looking is resting, hearing is a line. Things that ask nothing have no height at all: they're lines in the log, flat on the sheet.

Moving between the two is always visible. Answering anything sends it to its row, so you watch it filed rather than trust it vanished.

1 · What's asking, in order

Today: the sheet shows one decision card, the latest exhibit, the chart and the orders at one height. A second decision replaces the first card, and nothing shows where an answer went.

The sheet is the log on the left and the desk on the right. On the desk: a decision raised with its amber edge, two exhibits resting with dots, and a line of three reports. Slice 3's row counts them: one to decide, two to look at.

A second decision arrives. It slides under the first, which keeps the top because Mensah is idle on it. The top card shows +1, and slice 3 counts two to decide.

Making the first so shrinks it down into slice 3's row. A line "Decided · documents below Sign" appears at the top of the row's record. The second decision rises into its place.

Got it on the reports folds the line into the row as "Heard · 3 reports".

Opening the layout options grows the card in place over the desk below it; the composer steps aside with a count. Got it files it into the row as "Had", and the composer slides into first place.

Why. The highest thing is what to do first, so you never sort. Watching an answer file is what lets you trust the desk to empty. Each slice keeps what happened in it.

2 · Looking back at a slice

Today: slice 2 is a dot and a name on the chart. To recall what you decided in it, you read the ledger or ask the lead.

Slice 3 is underway with a decision on the desk. Slice 2 is a flat row: "3 decided · 2 shipped · 3 had".

Pressing slice 2 expands it in place in the log: its exhibits as thumbnails, what it shipped, what you decided and when, what it taught. The rows under it move down.

Taking out the fee table lifts it out of the row onto the desk, resting beside the composer, marked as from slice 2.

Got it sends it back down into slice 2's row.

Why. A slice is something you agreed, so its record should read as one. Taking a thing out puts it where things that ask you sit; putting it back is the same move as filing anything.

3 · A slice lands with something still asking

Today: the slice's PR merges and the chart moves on; the open question reads as if it belonged to the new slice.

Slice 3 is underway, with one decision on the desk homed to it.

Slice 3's pull request merges. Its row drops into the past as "landed just now", still counting one to decide in amber. Portal rises as slice 4, underway, asking nothing yet; a new next row appears above it.

The decision stays on top of the desk, now marked "slice 3", so it reads as the old slice's unfinished business.

Making it so files it into slice 3's row. The row's amber goes, and the desk is calm: nothing asking you, slice 4 underway.

Why. An answer belongs to the work that raised it. A slice that has landed can still be owed something; the log says so, and the desk keeps asking until you answer.

  1. Slice 3 underway: one decision asking
  2. It lands: slice 4 rises, slice 3 drops back
  3. Still owed: the decision stays, marked slice 3
  4. Make it so: it files home; the desk is calm
