# The ledger

`~/.focus/ledger.jsonl` is the ship's log of how work went, one JSON line per event, appended and never rewritten: QA verdicts, merges and closes (with QA's badge at that moment, so a merge without a QA pass shows), merge conflicts (with the files that conflicted, so the retro can see which keep colliding), merge checks, triage decisions, each goal as it closes, and a digest of each agent session as it ends. It outlives the reviews, agents and transcripts it's about: `state.json` drops a review when its PR merges and an agent when they go home, and Claude Code deletes transcripts after 30 days unless told otherwise.

## Reviewing it

Reviewing the ledger changes nothing: anything that gets decided is decided at the retro (with the `retro` plugin on). Focus serves it at `GET /api/ledger?days=`.

## A proper log

- **Numbered lines.** Each line starts with the format's version (`"v":2`) and carries a sequence number (`seq`), one more than the line before.
- **One writer.** `LedgerLog` (`server/ledgerLog.ts`) appends each line and flushes it to disk (fsync) before Focus carries on. A lock file beside it (`ledger.jsonl.lock`, holding Focus's process id) keeps a second Focus on the same data folder from writing too: that one reads the ledger, keeps its own new lines in memory, and says so on the report card (as a note, not as damage). A lock left by a Focus that's gone is taken over.
- **Checked whole on load** (`checkLedger`):
  - a last line cut short (Focus stopped mid-write) is dropped and the file trimmed;
  - damage in the middle (a line that doesn't read, numbers missing, repeated or going back) is never skipped quietly: Ops copies the file aside (`ledger.jsonl.damaged-<time>`, once: the same damage found on a later start points at the copy already made), says what's wrong at the top of the report card (**⚠ Ledger damaged**) and in its log, and carries on numbering from the highest line it read;
  - lines from the first format (before numbering) are numbered and the file rewritten once, the old one kept as `ledger.jsonl.v1-<time>`.

## Session digests

When an agent's session ends, Ops digests what's new in its Claude Code transcript into a `session-digest` line (`server/agents/sessionDigest.ts`), from code alone: no model reads it as it ends. Its frictions are read later, at retro time (below), by the retro plugin.

- **What:** what the session did and what it wasted (failed calls, refused calls, failed calls run again unchanged: running the tests again after an edit isn't waste), the captain's messages and how many were nudges, and what it cost since its last digest.
- **The captain's replies to leads:** beside each lead's digest goes a `captain-replies` line: every message the captain sent the lead in that span, in full, each with the end of what the lead had said since the last message to it. It's the record a chief of staff learns from: which of a lead's turns needed no more than "carry on", which a pick, and which the captain's own judgement. Transcripts go after 30 days, so these outlive them. As Focus starts, the leads' spans digested before replies were kept get theirs from the transcripts still on this machine (`backfillReplies`), marked `backfilled`; a span with none gets an empty line, so it isn't read again.
- **The captain's decisions:** each time the captain answers something waiting on them in their [inbox](#the-captains-inbox-api) (approves or declines a raised hand, picks a question's option, replies to a lead whose turn ended or acknowledges it, closes a goal ready to close) it gets a `captain-decision` line: what it was (`item`: its inbox id, kind and line), who it was for and their goal, what they decided in a few words (`answer`), and where (`via`: `office`, from Comms or an agent's panel, `api`, from a bot on the inbox's API, or `chief-of-staff`, its words sent by themselves in a project set to auto-send them). A message to a lead at work answers nothing in the inbox, and gets none; nor does an answer Ops came back on (to ask about the cost of picking a conversation back up, or warn before closing a goal). The inbox API serves the last two days' as `decided`; they're in the review's Sessions as `Decided: … → …`.
- **The chief of staff's verdicts:** with the [chief-of-staff plugin](../plugins/chief-of-staff/README.md) on for a project, each of its goal leads' turns gets a `cos-verdict` line as it ends: where the chief of staff would have sent it, why, and the words it would have sent; and where Jev picked the route (the plugin's Route with Jev), how it did, or why it couldn't. The captain's inbox files the turn by it (the plugin's README), and they're there to check against the `captain-replies` and `captain-decision` lines that follow.
- **QA's reports, graded:** with the [QA scores plugin](../plugins/qa-scores/README.md) on, some of QA's reports are graded by a model against the diff each reviewed, each a `qa-score` line: the label and score, why, the stamp QA's verdict had, and the scorer's digest and model.
- **Never twice:** each digest covers the transcript from the line after the last digest of that session to its end, and is keyed by the session and that last line. Calling it again with nothing new does nothing. A cloud session's transcript isn't on this machine, so it isn't digested.
- **Frictions, read at retro time** (`plugins/retro/frictions.ts`, with the `retro` plugin on; without it, none are read): when the captain starts a retro, the chief engineer reads each span in the window no one has read yet, finds the moments where Ops, its tools, the environment or the way of working got in the way, and gives each a kind from a fixed list and a short signature, reusing the signatures already in the ledger so the same trouble groups together. Every friction is quoted, and code drops any quote that isn't in that span's text word for word. What it found in each span (none included) goes in the ledger as a `session-frictions` line, so no span is read twice. It only finds and quotes: it doesn't judge the work, rank anything or give verdicts.
- **Why Opus, not Haiku per session:** digests were first read by Haiku as each session ended. A bake-off on 9 Oct over the same 76 sessions found both cost about the same per session (Haiku's calls carry Claude Code's overhead each time) and found about as many frictions, but Haiku gave the same trouble different words (86 signatures to Opus's 36: merge conflicts split three ways, so the groups a retro counts fell apart), and missed some Opus found. Digests Haiku read keep their frictions, and count like the rest.
- **Older sessions:** transcripts from before digests began can be digested from code alone, so it costs no Claude usage; each is dated when its session ended and marked `backfilled`.

## Intent

A project's or a goal's intent (what it's after, and its constraints) is a ledger line of its own (`intent`, read back in `shared/intents.ts`), not prose in a lead's brief or the inbox. Typed records are overwritten less than markdown when models keep them.

- **One intent format.** A project's intents are `intent` lines with no goal, several to a project, each by its `id`; a goal's names the goal, and the project intents it `serves`. Each change is a new line with every field again, never an edit. A change says `why`, and an intent is ended by a `retired` line, not by deleting it. Anything that keeps intents (a lead, the threads plugin) writes this line, never a format of its own.
- **Checked on load and on write.** An intent line that's missing a field or has one of the wrong shape is reported as damage like any other and left out, and Ops refuses to write one; `record` says why (`shared/intents.test.ts`).
- **No decision records.** Leads once raised decisions as `decision` lines, decided by the captain's pick, with rules against reopening one. It was dropped for what plain Claude Code does: a lead asks, and the answer lives in their conversation and brief. Old `decision` lines stay in the file and are left out as it loads (`shared/ledger.test.ts`).

## Did the retro's fixes work?

Each retro draft the captain accepts goes in the ledger with its frictions' signatures and the issue it was filed as. When a pull request merges, Ops notes the issues it closed, so the report card can tell when a fix landed, and for each accepted fix (`fixEffects` in `shared/ledger.ts`) compares how often its frictions came up before the captain accepted it and since it landed. The fixes are read from the ledger, so the report card keeps counting them with the retro plugin off.

## The captain's inbox API

What waits on the captain (what Comms flags on each goal) is also served to anything outside Focus, a bot answering for them, and answered the same way. The inbox is the [chief of staff](../plugins/chief-of-staff/README.md)'s (`plugins/chief-of-staff/captainsInbox.ts`, rules in `inbox.ts` and `inboxRules.ts` beside it): with it switched off there's no inbox, and nothing records the captain's decisions.

- `GET /api/plugins/chief-of-staff/inbox` gives `{ open, landed, decided }`. `open` is the queue, across the shown projects, most urgent first, read turns last: each item has an `id` that stays the same while it waits (`turn:<agent>:<when the turn ended>`, `question:<agent>:<question>`, `hand:<agent>:<request>`…), its `kind`, `projectId`, `agentId` (none for a pull request's), `who`, `goal`, `what`, `since`, `read` for a turn the captain has read, a question's `options`, `lane` (`needs-you`, `ready`, or `judging` while the chief of staff is still reading the turn), and `cos` for a lead's turn it has judged (its `route`, `ask`, `why`, `message`, and `replies`: the answers it suggests to what the lead asked, each a `label` for a button and the `text` to send as a `{ "text" }` decision). A turn the captain acknowledged isn't in it. `landed` is what merged itself in Show for the captain to hear of, newest first ([landed in Show](../plugins/chief-of-staff/README.md#landed-in-show)): each with an `id` (`landed:<project>:<repo>#<number>`), `projectId`, `repo`, `number`, `goal`, `leadId`, `who`, `at` (when it merged) and the chief of staff's `why`. `decided` is the last two days' `captain-decision` lines, newest first (with the `decision` each settled, if any).
- `POST /api/plugins/chief-of-staff/inbox/:id/decide` answers one, with one of `{ "allow": true|false }` (a raised hand), `{ "option": "b", "note": "…" }` (a question), `{ "text": "…" }` (a reply to a lead, a question answered in their own words, or a goal ready to close told what's left), `{ "close": true }` (a goal ready to close) `{ "send": true }` (a turn in the `ready` lane: the chief of staff's words go to the lead, from it) or `{ "ack": true }` (any lead's turn, Got it: it's read and off the queue until the lead's next turn, nothing is sent, the lead isn't woken, and the chief of staff's words for it no longer go by themselves; recorded with the answer `Acknowledged`, so it counts as the captain's answer). Send `how`: `"key"` to pick the conversation back up on the captain's API key, or `"wait"` until the plan's limit resets, when answering again after `{ costly }`. It's refused with a 400 saying why when the item's no longer waiting or the answer doesn't fit it; a pull request's is decided on the board. It gives `{ ok: true }`, or `{ costly }` when picking the lead's conversation back up would cost enough on the captain's API key to ask first, or `{ warn }` when closing the goal would leave work behind. It's recorded `via: "api"`.
- `POST /api/plugins/chief-of-staff/landed/:id/decide` answers one of `landed`, with `{ "ack": true }` (Got it: nothing is sent), `{ "redirect": "…" }` (the words go to its lead as the captain's) or `{ "revert": "…" }` (its lead hears to revert it as their next slice, the words as why; they may be empty). Words need its goal still open: a closed goal's is refused with a 400. Recorded as a `captain-decision` with the item's kind `landed`; it gives `{ ok: true }`.
- `POST /api/plugins/chief-of-staff/inbox/:id/hold` holds the chief of staff's words on a `ready` turn, in a project set to auto-send them (the manager panel's **Auto-send ready words**): they'd otherwise go by themselves two minutes after the verdict. Held, they wait for the captain like any other project's. It gives `{ ok: true }`.
- Each agent's item also carries what they said last (`said`) and what a lead showed on their screen this turn (`shown`: each one's `title`, its live page `html` and its picture `image`, paths under `/api/artifacts`).
- Like the rest of the API it's guarded by the host it's reached on (loopback, the Tailscale name, `FOCUS_ALLOWED_HOSTS`) and, over the tailnet, by who's asking: a device a person owns, not a tagged one ([who reaches Focus](settings.md#who-reaches-focus)).

### A bot speaking for the captain

A bot (Grok Bot, over Tailscale, on a tagged device that reaches nothing else of Focus's) gets the same inbox with a token of its own (`server/bot.ts` guards the routes, the chief of staff serves them). Each of these is set in the ready room's **Settings → Bot** (which makes the token, or takes the bot's own), taking effect at once, or by the environment variable named, which wins ([settings](settings.md)):

- The token (`FOCUS_BOT_TOKEN`): the bot sends it as `Authorization: Bearer <token>` to `GET /api/bot/inbox`, `POST /api/bot/inbox/:id/decide`, `POST /api/bot/inbox/:id/hold` and `POST /api/bot/landed/:id/decide`, which work as the routes above; its answers are recorded `via: "api"`. Without the token they answer 401, and with no token set, 404.
- The webhook (`FOCUS_BOT_WEBHOOK`): a URL Focus posts each change to, within a few seconds, as JSON with `event` and `at`:
  - `item-opened` with the `item`, as `GET` lists it: as soon as it needs the captain (a card or an interrupt the moment it's judged) or has the chief of staff's words ready; not while it's still being read. Turns still being read are already in `GET /api/bot/inbox` (`lane: "judging"`); no webhook fires for them until they're judged.
  - `item-closed` with its `id` and `how`: `decided`, with the `decision` (its `captain-decision` line), or `withdrawn` (it went another way: the lead carried on, the hand was withdrawn).
  - `shown`: a lead put something on their station's screen (a briefing, a mockup, a diagram), with `projectId`, `agentId`, who, the goal, its `title`, `html` and `image`.
  - `alert`: something a plugin raises to the captain directly, not through the chief of staff (the scheduler's watchdog: a lead hasn't answered a scheduled run), with `by` (the plugin), `agent`, `agentId`, the goal and its `text`, as soon as it's raised. It's an `alert` line in the ledger too. It's sent by the plugin that raises it, whether or not the chief of staff is on.
  Each is signed: `X-Focus-Signature: sha256=<hex>` is the HMAC-SHA256, keyed by the token, of `X-Focus-Timestamp` (milliseconds), a dot, and the body. What was waiting when Focus started, or when the bot was given its token and webhook, isn't sent (read the inbox for it), and a delivery that fails is logged, not sent again.
- Its header (`FOCUS_BOT_WEBHOOK_AUTH`): sent as the `Authorization` header on every delivery to that URL, so a receiver that needs its own token (a Grok Bot routine webhook) can take the post without a signature-checking proxy. The HMAC headers stay. If the value starts with `Authorization:`, that prefix is stripped (pasting a full header line works). Unset, deliveries are as they were.
- The public address (`FOCUS_PUBLIC_URL`): Focus as the bot reaches it, through Tailscale Serve (`https://<machine>.<tailnet>.ts.net`, no port: Serve answers on 443, and Focus itself listens only on loopback), so the links in what's sent are whole.
