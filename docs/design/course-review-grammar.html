Course review grammarComms · brownfield · a goal reviewed as its course and the waypoints on it

The job: small pull requests are right and each runs CI, but every one also lands on the user's list of things to see through to production, and there are now a lot of them. A goal is on a course with its waypoints in order; the user reviews the course at a waypoint, and never manages a pull request or starts a preview. The engine keeps every waypoint built on the one before, keeps a holodeck running across the repos at a waypoint, sends an away mission into that same holodeck on video, and routes each anomaly it brings back: to the lead, or to the user. All of it is data the engine holds. In Comms the course opens as a panel beside the sheet, and each thing in it opens the next panel to its right, one level at a time; Comms starts no work of its own.

The mockups and specimens are drawn in Comms' own look, dark, with the side panels grammar's tokens; the course is one card in its stack (option B, with Tanaka to build). The words are the ship's: course and waypoint are already what the star chart calls a goal's path and its slices (course.ts, Waypoint). Brownfield: the starting points are server/previews.ts, shared/types/goals.ts, shared/types/qa.ts and plugins/chief-of-staff/stream.ts as built on 30 October 2026. The demo is a made-up shop.

Assumptions

  • One user, Comms open for hours. The sheet stays quiet: the course is there when they open it, and only what's brought to them rises.
  • How the work is sliced is the lead's call: waypoints side by side when they're independent (a frontend and a backend change can already share one), stacked only when one needs the one before. The engine sets no order and no cap; it only shows them.
  • Pull requests stay: one per repo per waypoint, each running CI, QA's diff review and the ratchet script. They leave the user's list, not GitHub.
  • The holodeck runs on this machine, as previews do today. A project with several repos already has one command that brings them all up ({dir:<repo>}).
  • The side panels (option B) and the two one-tap buttons ("Didn't need me", "You should have asked me") ship first. The course is a card in that stack, and its levels cascade inside the card's width.

Settled with the user, 30 October

  • B, the course as a panel that cascades to the right. A and C stay below for comparison.
  • The ship's words: course, waypoint, holodeck, away mission (and its log), anomaly, last watch, make it so, belay.
  • A pull request opens as a fourth panel in the cascade, rather than jumping out to GitHub.
  • Slicing is the lead's call. The engine sets no order and no cap on open waypoints; the chief of staff says when work has waited on you a while.
  • Lanes carry over: a waypoint takes its riskiest pull request's lane; Ship and Show waypoints land themselves once what they depend on has; only Ask waypoints wait for Make it so.
  • One holodeck per project for now. Several come with sandboxes, where each agent session runs on its own machine with its own holodeck.
  • The ratchet script runs before QA; GitHub's merge queue where a repo has CI, Focus's own merge check where it doesn't, and the conflict-resolving agent stays; a cheap Done check at close compares Done looks like with what was delivered.
  • Nothing is left to confirm.

Today's flow and the proposed one

One goal on the demo shop, "Show stock on the shop": five changes across two repos, seven pull requests. Amber marks what lands on the user.

Today
  1. Lead works one slice at a time, a pull request per repo; the next slice starts when this one's have all merged or closed.
  2. CI · QA each pull request is reviewed on its own. QA starts its own dev server, checks out sibling pull requests beside it, and may record a video.
  3. Chief of staff reads the lead's turn and puts a decision on the sheet. The pull requests are rows under More, each with its own merge press.
  4. You to try it, pick a pull request on the ship's board and press Preview. One preview per project: starting another stops this one. Other repos' refs are guessed from feature labels.
  5. You merge each pull request, in the right order, and keep the rest in your head until they've all landed.
Proposed
  1. Lead plots waypoints as it sees fit, side by side or one on another, across repos; a pull request per repo per waypoint still runs CI, the ratchet script and QA's diff review.
  2. Engine keeps the holodeck running at the course's tip: every repo at the newest ready waypoint's commits.
  3. QA sends an away mission into that holodeck for each waypoint, on video, and logs each bug or gap as an anomaly.
  4. Chief of staff routes each anomaly: a plain bug goes back to the lead, quietly, with "You should have asked me" beside it; a gap that's a choice comes to you as a decision.
  5. You open the course whenever you like: what's new since your last watch, a mission's log, the holodeck at any waypoint. Make it so to a waypoint and the engine merges every pull request on the way, in order.

CourseThe lead plots waypoints; each lands on the course as it's pushed, then is built, loaded and surveyed. Whenever you open the course, the waypoints above your last watch are new. You open one, play its mission's log, load the holodeck there, and make it so.

HolodeckThe holodeck runs at the course's tip. Load here on waypoint 3 resets it there, every repo at waypoint 3's commits; it stays held until you make it so or follow the course again, and then follows the tip.

Governing principle

A thing sits as high as the attention it asks of you right now, and the higher it sits, the sooner it has to leave.

Comms' own principle, kept as the side panels grammar keeps it. The course asks nothing until you open it, so the sheet only carries its count. Opened, it is a panel beside the sheet. Each thing you open from it asks more of you than what you opened it from, so it sits a step higher and further right, and the one before folds back to a spine; it's the first to leave when you go back.

An anomaly brought to you is a decision on the sheet with an amber edge, as decisions are. An anomaly fixed without you never rises: it's a row in its waypoint. The holodeck is not in Comms at all; you enter it in a browser tab, because walking through the app is the thing itself, not a view of it.

Three ways to show a course in Comms

The same nouns, verbs and engine; only where the course lives changes. Each is acted out below.

B · As a panel that cascades Recommended

The sheet stays as it is today. Course on its head opens the course as a panel. Each thing in it opens the next panel to its right, one level at a time, and the panels before fold to spines you can go back to.

Nothing on the sheet changes, and no panel holds more than one level.

A · On the sheet

The goal's sheet shows its course under the head: the holodeck line, the waypoints, your last watch, and Make it so. Decisions rest under it. A waypoint opens as a panel.

Always in sight, and the sheet gets busy.

C · Only through decisions

No view of the course. When waypoints are ready, the chief of staff puts one decision on the sheet ("Make it so to waypoint 4?") mentioning them.

Quietest, but there's nothing to look at until you're asked.

Recommendation: B, the course as a panel that cascades

B keeps today's quiet: the sheet doesn't change, and the course is one press away whenever you want it. The first draft of B crammed a whole step into one panel; cascading fixes that. Each panel holds one level: the course is a list of waypoints, a waypoint is a line of what it is and one row each for its pull requests, its away mission and its anomalies, and only the panel after that shows a log or a still. You always see the way back, as spines on the left.

It stays inside the side panels' card width however deep you go, so the sheet never narrows or gets covered, which was the 28 October rule and the reason the side panels grammar turned down columns. It's still one card in that stack: Talk or a pull request opened from a decision stack over it as they do there; going deeper into the course moves right, inside it.

A puts the course in sight all the time, and the sheet gets busy with it. C keeps Comms exactly as quiet as today, but you only see work you've been asked about.

In all, about 12 to 15 pull requests, with several holodecks left to sandboxes. Ongoing cost: one away mission per waypoint rather than one browser pass per pull request, so fewer QA sessions for the same work; logs go where QA's screenshots already go.

Prove first

  1. Restacking after a squash merge. Squashing waypoint 3 leaves waypoint 4's branch carrying 3's old commits. A spike: two scratch repos, three stacked waypoints, squash the first, move the rest on with rebase --onto, and count what the conflict resolver would be handed. Half a day; everything rests on it.
  2. Loading the holodeck at a waypoint is quick enough. Point the demo shop's preview command at given commits in both repos and time a reload. If it's minutes, taking turns will grate, and sandboxes move up the list.
  3. A lead can run two waypoints ahead without tangling them. Try it on one real goal with instructions alone, before any engine change.
  4. Logs help once they're seen. The research goal found recordings didn't lower the user's attention, and that they weren't visible in Comms. Measure again once logs play in the mission's panel, with the one-tap buttons as the measure.

Order

  1. The two spikes above.
  2. Waypoints as data, then stacked branches: leads can work ahead, and each waypoint's pull requests still run CI.
  3. Holodeck at a waypoint: the engine keeps one per project at the tip.
  4. Away missions in that holodeck, then anomalies and their route.
  5. The Comms view, once the side panels have shipped.
  6. Make it so; the merge queue when the repos have CI.
  7. Sandboxes, as a goal of their own: a holodeck per agent session.
