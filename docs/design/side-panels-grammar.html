Side panels grammarComms · brownfield · what a decision mentions, opened beside it

The job: when Comms asks the user to decide something (merge this pull request, pick this layout), they want to look at what the decision is about without leaving it. Every pull request, exhibit or page a decision mentions becomes a link that opens it in a panel on the right, beside the decision, never over it. Panels gather as things are opened, with a way to move between them and close them. The engine already holds every one of these things as data; a panel only renders what it holds, so opening one starts no work and closing one loses nothing. The nouns and verbs below are fixed; three ways of stacking the panels differ only in their layer rules, and the Layers tab acts each one out and recommends one.

Drawn in Comms' own look, dark, rather than a light spec sheet: the colours, fonts, lifts and springs are bridge.css's tokens and bridgeKit.ts's pieces, value for value, so each mockup reads as Comms with one rule changed. Brownfield: the starting point is plugins/chief-of-staff/stream.ts as built on 29 October 2026.

Assumptions

  • One user, one screen, Comms open for hours: a sovereign app, so dense chrome is fine, but the sheet with its decision stays the thing they look at most.
  • A decision mentions two or three things at most. Four open panels is a lot; past that the oldest closes, since it's only a view.
  • Panels belong to the goal at the conn. Taking another goal's conn puts them away; the engine still holds everything they showed.
  • The sheet never slides or narrows to make room (the user's 28 October rule: it made it bob). Panels open where the Talk panel opens today.
  • Merge in a panel is the merge press Comms already has (mergePress): same label, same guard, same confirm.

To confirm

  • Which of A, B or C. The page recommends B.
  • Should an exhibit opened from the Exhibits list open in a panel too, rather than on the sheet as it does today? The grammar says yes (one way to look at a thing); it changes the 28 October choice.
  • A diff summary needs line counts GitHub has but Focus doesn't fetch (additions, deletions, changedFiles). Fetch them, or show QA's file list without counts?
  • Keys: Esc closes the front panel (as it puts Talk away now), [ and ] move between panels, G opens the front one on GitHub. Merge keeps no key: M is the muster's in Comms.
  • Should the stack survive a reload, as the conn does, or start empty?

Operational principles

MentionA decision names what it's about. You press a mention and the thing opens in a panel beside the decision; its slot lights. Press it again and the same panel comes forward, never a second one.
Pull requestYou open #9 from the merge ask. Its panel says whether the checks pass, whether it merges cleanly, what QA found and where to look. You look at the line on GitHub if you want to, come back, and merge from the panel. The decision fades as answered.
PanelA panel shows a thing the engine holds, as it is now. You read it, move to another, close it. Closing it loses nothing: open the mention again and it's back as it was.
StackPanels gather beside the sheet as you open things. The one you're reading is in front; the others stay in reach. Esc closes the front one; taking another goal's conn puts them all away.

Governing principle

A thing sits as high as the attention it asks of you right now, and the higher it sits, the sooner it has to leave.

It's Comms' own principle (README.md#the-watch), kept as is. The decision rests on the sheet and stays until it's answered. What it mentions sits a layer higher, in a panel beside it, because you look at it now and then put it away; it never covers the decision, since the decision is what you're there for. Of the panels, the one you're reading asks the most of you, so it sits highest and is the first to go.

The least demanding things stay low and still: the rail, the sheet, the decision. The most demanding are a hail, which sits over everything and leaves the moment it's answered or stood by, and the zoom, the one overlay, which covers Comms only while the user picks between a question's pictures.

Three ways to stack panels

The same nouns and verbs; only the Panel layer's rules change. Each is acted out below with the same eight steps.

A · Tabs in one panel

One panel at today's width. Each thing opened is a tab along its top; the tab picked shows. Close a tab with its ×; closing the last closes the panel.

Height: every tab sits at the same height. The order is the order opened; switching doesn't change it, and closing lands on the neighbour.

Talk: replaced, in effect. It's the panel's first tab, pinned; Discuss or T picks it.

B · A stack of cards Recommended

Each thing opened is a card that slides in over the last. The ones behind step back and leave their heads showing above it as spines: name and state. Press a spine to bring it forward; × or Esc pops the front one and you're back where you were.

Height: the last looked at is on top, and goes first. Width never grows past today's panel.

Talk: stacks with them, a card like any other; Discuss puts it on top with the decision quoted.

C · Columns

Each thing opened pushes in as a column from the right; the one you're reading is wide, the others fold to strips with their names down them, and breadcrumbs trace the order. Press a strip to widen it; close collapses a column.

Height: all on one layer, side by side. Each panel costs a strip of width.

Talk: pushed, to a strip of its own on the left.

Recommendation: B, a stack of cards

B is the principle drawn literally. The panel you're reading sits highest because it asks the most of you now, and it's the first to leave: Esc or × pops it and you're back at the one you read before, which is how the merge ask goes (look at the exhibit, back to the PR, merge). Its spines keep every panel's name and state in sight, so "QA passed" and "merged" read without opening anything.

A flattens height: the hidden tabs sit as high as the shown one, so the stack can't say which you were just reading, and closing lands on a neighbour rather than where you were. The user also took the lead's workspace tabs out of Comms on 28 October. C spends width: every panel adds a strip toward the decision, so on a 1280 or 1440 screen the second strip already crosses the sheet's edge, and the third covers the decision's text, unless the sheet moves, which the user ruled out on 28 October. B stays at today's panel width however many are open; four is the most, and the oldest closes for a fifth, since it's only a view.

Scenarios 1 to 3 are the same eight steps in A, B and C: open a pull request from a merge ask, read it, open an exhibit the decision mentions, move between the two, jump to GitHub, merge from the panel, close the panels.
