# Goals and their leads

A holodeck works on up to four goals at once, one per **team station**. Each goal has a lead of its own, a lieutenant in command red: your own Claude Code session for that goal. Open the holodeck's goals with <kbd>L</kbd> anywhere on it, **Goals** on its board, or its button in [Comms](comms.md) (<kbd>C</kbd>), which also lists every goal under way on the ship and hails you when a lead is waiting for you; <kbd>E</kbd> on a goal's team station (or its lead) opens the lead's panel. Each goal shows its lead, its slice and its pull requests: **Talk** opens the lead's panel, where you talk to them like any Claude Code session. To start another, tell the box at the bottom what you're after and pick how hard the lead thinks (**Effort a new goal's lead starts at** in Settings sets the default, high to begin with). A lead of its own takes the first free station, or the one you walked up to: <kbd>E</kbd> on a free station opens the goals list to start a goal there. However you start one (here, or **To a goal** from the New issue stand or Number One), a toast says which station its lead took, with **Beam there** to go straight to its briefing room.

The **team stations** are four holo-pods, two along each side wall, like the ones the Lower Decks crew train in: a cream shell with a ribbed ring in the station's colour on top, and a door through a thick bezel at the front. Over each floats a holographic sign, like the ones over the holodeck pods in Lower Decks: the station, its goal and lead, and how far through the slice they are (its checklist ticked off, as a percentage), edged in how the room stands. The lamps either side of the door say the same at a glance: dark while the station's free, breathing cyan while the lead's at work, pulsing gold while they need you, steady green when it's your turn, red once they've stopped. Each door opens onto the goal's **briefing room**, which stands somewhere else entirely: it's drawn through the open door from where you'd be standing on the other side, and stepping over the threshold moves you there, facing the same way, so you just walk in (and out again the same way). Round its table sit the lead (at your right hand as you take the captain's chair at its head), the goal's reviewer across from them and their helpers down either side, each with a PADD, all facing the big screen on the far wall with the lead's terminal, browser and progress. The **goal board** on the starboard wall lists who's round the table like the crew leaderboard in Lower Decks, each with a portrait of their own character, name, part and what they're doing, beside the lead's note on the goal: this slice's checklist, what done looks like and what might come next (before there's a note, the goal as you first put it). Press <kbd>E</kbd> on anyone at the table, or on their row on the goal board, for their terminal (a helper's transcript), or anywhere else on the board for the lead's goal and to-dos. Teleporting to a lead puts you at the head of the table. A free station's screen, sign and board say it's free, or which goal is next in line. Goals show as in progress on the holodeck's board too, whether or not their lead is at work right now: click one for its lead's panel, or **Beam there** on it for its briefing room.

- **Their workspace:** every repo of the project, checked out side by side at `./<repo>` on the slice's branch (`focus/<goal>-<slice>-<id>`), so a change that runs through the API, the web app and the admin app is just one piece of work. They read each repo's CLAUDE.md, run its tests and check UI changes in the browser, like the rest of the crew.
- **How they work:** talk a goal through briefly, then build it a slice at a time. A slice is a reasonably sized change that ships on its own: often a thin slice end to end through every repo it needs, or a refactor once the code has learned something.
- **Their helpers:** when they hand independent pieces of work to subagents (Claude Code's Agent tool), each one sits down at the lead's table as an ensign with a PADD while it works: its bubble shows what it's doing (from Claude Code's progress summaries, about every 30 seconds, and its tool calls). It says how it got on when it reports back, then leaves. Four seats; any more wait for one. They're listed under **Crew** in the lead's panel too. Press E on one, or click it under Crew, to see its own transcript: what the lead asked of it, its tool calls and what it reported back, without the lead's conversation round it (the lead's terminal shows only the Agent call and its answer). It's kept in memory while the helper's at the station, and stays up in an open panel after it leaves.
- **Shipping a slice:** they never push. When a slice is ready they ask the office to ship it (`mcp__office__ship`, which in **ask** mode raises their hand for you to approve first). The office pushes every repo with new commits since it last shipped and opens a pull request in each (or updates the one it opened), and the holodeck's QA officer reviews them. Nothing ships while any repo has uncommitted changes. Like a developer's push, it fetches with `--prune` first and rides out a few GitHub 5xx answers (see [agents](agents.md)).
- **Hearing back:** a QA fail goes straight to them, and they fix it and ship again, twice at most before it's yours to look at. A pass is passed on with your next message. You merge their pull requests from the board (they don't merge by themselves). Once every pull request of the slice has merged or closed, they hear about it, and their repos move to fresh code on a new branch for the next slice at the start of their next turn. A lead that's idle wakes for it and tells you what the slice taught them; one in the middle of a turn is told to carry on where they are (`landedMidTurnNote` in `lead.ts`), since their commits come along, and to share what it taught them when they next report. A repo with uncommitted changes holds that back, so nothing is lost. Commits they made after shipping come along: the office cherry-picks them onto the new branch, and when they don't apply cleanly, they stay on the old branch and the lead is told how to bring them over (`carriedNote` in `lead.ts`). A branch never ships again once a pull request from it has merged: a squash merge leaves its own commits off the base, so a new pull request from it would carry them again and conflict. When the lead ships a repo whose pull request merged while the slice's others are still open (or the next slice is held back), the office first moves that repo to a new branch from fresh code, cherry-picking only the commits that never shipped, and tells the lead (`needsFreshBranch` and `freshBranchNote` in `lead.ts`). If they don't apply cleanly, nothing ships and the lead hears how to bring them over.
- **The goal's note:** the lead keeps a short note on the goal through the office (`mcp__office__note`): what done looks like, this slice's checklist (ticked off as they go), a line or two on what each slice taught them, and up to three ideas for what's next. It's a note, not a spec: the office refuses one that grows too long (`checkNote` in `lead.ts`), and the tool's schema (`noteShape` in `officeTool.ts`) tells the lead each limit up front, from the same `NOTE_LIMITS`, and clears the checklist when a slice lands. It shows on the goal in the goals list and above the lead's terminal, and the lead hears it again at each new slice, so it survives their conversation being summarised. They can retitle the goal through it too. The office also keeps your first words on the goal, and the lead hears them with the note at each new slice and when you compact their conversation, or it has to start afresh.
- **Their context:** a goal can run for a long time in one conversation, and when to lighten it is your call: a lead may still be in the middle of something when a slice lands, so the office never compacts it by itself. The **Context** readout in an agent's panel (and the status bar of their display, and the goal's card) shows how full their context was at the end of their last turn, gold from 60% and red from 80%; **Compact** beside it compacts it now, between turns, for any agent with a conversation on this machine (Claude Code's `/compact`; for a lead, with instructions to keep the goal, what done looks like, the decisions and why, the lessons, your preferences and the PRs, and to drop file contents, diffs and tool output: `compactInstructions` in `lead.ts`). Claude Code still compacts by itself when a context fills up. Leads are also told to hand broad exploration to subagents and keep only their conclusions.
- **How hard they think:** the effort picker in their panel changes it any time: from their next request if they're working, otherwise from their next turn. Each change costs a little, since the prompt cache starts over.
- **One per goal:** their record lasts as long as the goal, from slice to slice. Their instructions say who they are and how they work, and stay the same for the whole goal (Claude Code keeps a session's first system prompt when it resumes); where their repos are, and on which branch, comes in messages. Other leads may be working in the same repos at the same time, each on their own branches.
- **Showing you things:** the lead can put a visual on their station's big screen (`mcp__office__show`): a diagram, a mockup, options side by side, as a self-contained HTML page laid out for 1280 × 800. The office takes a picture of it in a headless browser for the screen, which shows the one shown last in place of the browser pane. Press E on the screen to look at each of them up close, live (the page runs sandboxed in an origin of its own, so its scripts can't reach the office), and switch between them down the sidebar. Showing an id again replaces it. They stay with the goal when it closes: its card under **Closed goals** has a **Shown** button that opens them the same way, read-only, with the questions you answered and what you picked (and any you hadn't). Reopening the goal puts them back on its screen. They go once the goal drops off the end of the room's log.
- **Explaining things:** when you need to understand how something works (a mechanism, what happens when it fails, how data moves, why one design beats another), the lead walks you through it with an animated explainer rather than paragraphs (`mcp__office__explain`). They give its beats, each a caption and how long it plays, and a stage that draws the whole scene from the time t; the office wraps them in the ship's LCARS player (`explainerPage` in `explainer.ts`): an elbow frame, the beats as numbered blocks down the left, the caption under the stage, and ◀ Step, Play and Step ▶ with a timeline along the bottom. It goes up like any of show's visuals, sharing their ids. Until you press **Run briefing** it rests, dimmed, on its last frame, so the screen's picture sums it up. Up close, Play runs it through, Step (or ← and →) plays one beat and pauses, and clicking a beat's block or its part of the timeline jumps there. The player runs the stage through every moment as it loads, so a stage that throws puts a Stage fault panel up, and the lead hears about it: the office passes on whatever errors a page reports while it takes the picture, for show's visuals too (`pageErrorsNote` in `lead.ts`).
- **Asking you with pictures:** for a question best answered by looking (which layout, which diagram reads best), the lead asks it with options, each a page of its own with a line or two on what it trades off, and says why they're asking (`mcp__office__ask`). The screen shows the question in large type over the lettered options, the briefing room's sign says Your answer, and it counts towards Needs you in Comms and the Tricorder. Press E on the screen to look at them up close: one at a time, large, with its detail and Pick button beside it (← and → or the lettered tabs step through them, and each page stays loaded, so you keep your place), or all at once with Side by side. Pick one, with a note if you like: your pick goes to the lead as a message from you. Asking again replaces a question you haven't answered; answered ones stay in the sidebar, marked with your pick, for the rest of the goal.
- **Ending a goal:** when what done looks like is met, or a slice lands and nothing worthwhile is left, the lead says so (`mcp__office__propose_close`): the goal shows Ready to wrap up, and you close it, or carry on talking. You can close a goal any time with **Close goal** in the goals list, or in the lead's panel (up with its links, next to **Board**, well away from **Send**). Closing never throws work away: uncommitted changes are committed as a WIP snapshot on the goal's branch before its checkouts go (and if that fails, the goal stays open). It asks first, in an in-game dialog, if that leaves work only in the office's clone (a snapshot, commits never pushed, a branch that never reached GitHub), leaves pull requests open with no lead, or closes a goal that shipped nothing; a checkout git can't look at counts as having changes. A closed goal goes in the holodeck's log (under **Closed goals** in the goals list, with what it taught you, its branch and every slice's pull requests, each marked with the slice it shipped in), its lead goes home and its station is free. **Reopen** brings the same lead back to a free station, with every repo on the goal's branch as it was left (the WIP snapshot at its tip), and picks their conversation back up.
- **When every station is taken:** a new goal, whether you start it in the goals list or hand it over from the New issue stand or Number One, waits in line under **Waiting for a team station**, where you can reorder it or take it out. Goals in line never start by themselves: once a station is free, the first one shows **Start it**, with its words and effort ready to change first.

Leads don't take one of the holodeck's two individual desks or count towards its agent limit (at most two: one per desk). They do count towards **Agents working at once** while they work: a message to a lead (or any agent) that has finished its turn waits for a free slot like anyone starting work.
