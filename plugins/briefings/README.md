# Briefings

A plugin (`FOCUS_PLUGINS=briefings`, [plugins](../../docs/plugins.md)), switched on in each project's settings too (the manager's **Projects** tab, under **Plugins**): a goal's lead can walk the captain through a concept with an animated explainer on their station's screen, narrated in their voice up close. Without it, a lead's office has no `explain` tool, and the screen shows what they put up with show, model and ask as it always does; explainers already on a screen still play (they're self-contained pages), with no Voice button.

## The explainer

for a concept rather than a code path, when you need to understand how something works (a mechanism, what happens when it fails, how data moves, why one design beats another), the lead walks you through it with an animated explainer rather than paragraphs (`mcp__office__explain`). They give its beats, each a caption and how long it plays, and a stage that draws the whole scene from the time t; the office wraps them in the ship's LCARS player (`explainerPage` in `explainer.ts`): an elbow frame, the beats as numbered blocks down the left, the caption under the stage, and ◀ Step, Play and Step ▶ with a timeline along the bottom. It goes up like any of show's visuals, sharing their ids. Until you press **Run briefing** it rests, dimmed, on its last frame, so the screen's picture sums it up. The briefing room's screen shows that picture, like any other visual's, and redraws only when something changes. Up close it's the live page: Play runs it through, Step (or ← and →) plays one beat and pauses (its caption staying up over its last frame), and clicking a beat's block or its part of the timeline jumps there. Up close, **Voice** (along the bottom, off until you switch it on, and remembered by the browser) has the lead talk you through it: each beat has a narration they wrote for it (or, if they left one out, its title and caption read out), said in the lead's own voice by Kokoro on this machine (see [Number One's voice mode](../number-one/README.md#voice-mode), whose voice kit it shares), while the beat plays. The voice sets the pace: a beat holds on its last frame until its line's finished, then the next one plays. The held frame is drawn a moment before the next beat's start, so a stage that switches scene as a beat starts doesn't show the next one while the line's still being said (`player.test.ts`). Pause pauses the voice too and Play picks it up; Step, a beat's block or the timeline cut it short and start afresh. The page runs sandboxed, so it can't reach the office itself: it posts each line to the screen panel around it, where the plugin's part of the screen hears it (`narrator.ts`, put round the page through the core's `screen` hook in `client.ts`), which fetches the first beats' audio as soon as the voice is on, and each next beat's as one plays: only a beat ahead, since Kokoro says one line at a time for the whole office (the core's `server/speech.ts`), and lines wanted now (Number One's voice mode, say) go ahead of lines fetched ahead of time (`nextLine`). A line nobody's waiting for any more (hushed, or its page closed) is dropped from the queue. A beat whose line isn't ready yet (Kokoro still loading, say) holds until it is: the browser's own voice says it only if Kokoro has failed, and then the best of the system's voices (a premium or neural one if there is, never a novelty or robotic one; `crewVoice.ts`). Once Kokoro's downloaded, the office loads it as it starts (not in the demo office), so the first line is quick too. If they explain again under the same id (asked for more on a part, say), the new version replaces the briefing and says what changed: the screen compares its beats' lines and titles with the version before (`firstChangedBeat` in `shared.ts`), puts **Updated · beat N changed** by its title, and the player waits at the start of that beat (a `show` message), to play from there rather than from the top. The player runs the stage through every moment as it loads, so a stage that throws puts a Stage fault panel up, and the lead hears about it: the office passes on whatever errors a page reports while it takes the picture, for show's visuals too (`pageErrorsNote` in the core's `lead.ts`). Every explainer gets looked at for its lead: in the same visit as the screen's picture, the office jumps to the middle of each beat (`kitSeek`) and takes a picture of the stage (`LeadToolContext.showFrames`), the player says how flat it is (`kitSeen`), and the tool checks that (`momentNotes`): a stage that's one flat colour has nothing on it. The explain tool's reply carries those pictures and what the checks found, so the lead sees their explainer as the captain will and fixes it before you open it.

## The writer

A lead can send a brief instead of the beats and stage: what to explain and to whom, the decision they need, and where its facts are. The office's writer (`writer.ts`) writes the explainer from it in a read-only session that reads the lead's code and their last three messages, with the same instructions a lead gets (`EXPLAIN_CRAFT`), explained bottom up with the simplest step-by-step animations. Its receipts are checked as a lead's are; what doesn't hold up goes back to the same session, twice at most, before the lead hears of it. The lead's reply says who wrote it and what it took ("Written from your brief by Opus 5.5 on fast mode at low effort, 70 s, $0.30").

The captain picks the writer on the engineering panel's **Briefings** card, for the whole office: the model (the leads' own, or Opus 5.5, Sonnet 5.5 or Haiku 4.5), its effort (the leads', or a level), and **Fast**, Opus's fast mode: quicker, at twice the price per token, and left off on a model without it (`writerRun`). The card says what the last brief cost to write up. The setting is the plugin's saved state, changed through `POST /api/plugins/briefings/writer` (`writerPatch` checks it).

## Receipts

Each beat can say where its facts come from (`refs`): a file in the lead's checkout, the lead's own words earlier in their session, a line of the ship's log by its number, or a pull request (`owner/repo#number`: its title, description, comments, QA's report among them, and reviews, read through `gh`), each with the source's words quoted exactly. Before the explainer goes up, the explain tool looks each one up (`sources.ts`) and checks it (`receipts.ts`): the quote has to be in its source, word for word give or take spacing and case, and every number and name the beat's caption and narration say has to be in one of its quotes (its title, a heading, isn't checked). Numbers count in figures or in words from eleven up ("five hundred and fifty five", and "thirty five cents" as 0.35). Names are capitalised words (a sentence's first word too, unless it's a common word like "So" or a contraction) and anything in code style, but not titles or the office's own words. If anything doesn't hold up, nothing goes on the screen, and the lead hears beat by beat what to fix. So a briefing about how Focus itself works cites the code as it is: a file's receipt names the line the quote is on and the commit the checkout is at, and says if the file has changed since. Up close, a beat's receipts are chips under its caption, never read aloud. Tapping one opens its quote over the stage. A beat with no refs gets an **Interpretation** chip: it's the lead's reading, not something the office checked.

## Where it lives

`plugins/briefings/`: `server.ts` (the plugin: its lead tool and the writer setting's route), `explainTool.ts` (the `explain` tool: its input, its description and what it answers), `explainer.ts` (the LCARS player, pure), `receipts.ts` (checking a beat against its quotes, pure), `sources.ts` (where the quotes are looked up), `writer.ts` (the office's writer, which writes a brief up), `demo.ts` (the explainer the demo's scripted leads put up), `shared.ts` (the writer setting, pure), `client.ts` (its part of the station's screen up close, and the **Briefings** card), `narrator.ts`, `voice.ts` (whether the voice is on, per browser) and `briefings.css`.
