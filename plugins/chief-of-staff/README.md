# The chief of staff

A plugin (`FOCUS_PLUGINS=chief-of-staff`, [plugins](../../docs/plugins.md)), switched on per project too (the manager's **Projects** tab, under **Plugins**: **Chief of staff**). It keeps the captain's inbox: what waits on them across the shown projects (Comms, with its badge on the HUD and its strips on the phone, and a bot on the [inbox API](../../docs/ledger.md#the-captains-inbox-api)), and what they decided, each answer a `captain-decision` line, whether it came from Comms, an agent's panel or the bot. As a goal's lead ends a turn, it judges where that turn goes, writes its verdict in the [ledger](../../docs/ledger.md#session-digests), and marks the turn with it (`lead.marks`), so the inbox files the turn by it. And on [its direct line](#the-direct-line) in Comms, tied to no goal, the user talks things over with it, and it starts a goal itself when the conversation calls for one, saying so. It needs no other plugin.

## What it does with a turn

It sends nothing itself unless the captain lets it, project by project (but for the news it [wakes a lead for](#the-leads-news-it-wakes-them-for), and what it starts or tells from [its direct line](#the-direct-line), where the user talks with it). What it decides is where a lead's ended turn waits for them:

- **While it reads the turn** (up to a minute: `JUDGING_MS`, `shared.ts`) the turn is held back: off the badge, no toast, a line in Comms saying how many it's reading. A turn it hasn't judged by then needs the captain as if it weren't there, so a judge that's slow, failing or switched off never hides one.
- **A card or an interrupt** (routes 2 and 3) needs the captain: it's on the badge, headed by its one line on what's being decided and its words to the captain; the lead's own words and its reason are folded under them, since the captain asked for less to read (the lead's room is a press away, B, for more). As it's judged, a toast says so: an interrupt as an error, so it sounds like one.
- **Carry on, or back to the lead** (routes 1 and 4) are **Ready to send**: off the badge and quiet, with its words for the lead. The captain sends them with one press (the lead hears them `[Chief of Staff]`), or replies in their own words instead. In a project set to **Auto-send ready words** (the manager's **Projects** tab, beside its switch once it's on there; a project set so before the setting was the chief of staff's keeps it) its words sending a turn back go by themselves two minutes after the verdict (`AUTO_SEND_MS`), counted down on its card at the end of the goal's thread in Comms and on the phone's Ready to send, unless the captain holds them; recorded `via: "chief-of-staff"`. Words carrying a lead on never go by themselves: in the defence backtest, its own reading of "agreed" overrode the captain's holds ("Not yet, let me review the plan first") and their new requirements, so carrying on stays the lead's call by its instructions, or the captain's (`lanes.test.ts`, `captainsInbox.test.ts`).
- **Suggested replies, and Got it.** On any route it suggests up to three replies to what the lead actually asked or offered ("Keep it open", "Close it"), a button each on the turn's card in Comms: pressed, its words go to the lead as the captain's own reply would.
- **A turn that asks several things is an ask each.** The judge gives one ask per decision, each with its own headline and replies, since one headline squashed them ("Merge api#1287, and pick the next slice: shared link or emails?") and a button could only answer all of it at once. The card shows a block per ask, answered by a reply or in the user's own words; each answer is held, shown and changeable, and nothing goes to the lead until every ask has one, then all of them go as one message, each under its ask's headline (`askRules.ts`). Held answers are kept on the turn's read (`CosRead.answers`), so a reload or a restart keeps them; Got it still clears the whole turn and sends nothing. Its inbox headline is the first ask and how many more. It makes no ask of merging a pull request the card has a merge button for: it's told the lead's open pull requests (`context.ts`), and the merge is answered there. A verdict from before, with one `ask`, reads as a list of one. **Got it** on every lead's turn (on its card, and on the phone's Ready to send) acknowledges it: read, off the queue until the lead's next turn, nothing sent, and its words no longer counting down. Ops' news after it (a slice landing, QA's verdict) carries the lead on, since the captain is owed nothing. It's recorded as the captain's decision (`Acknowledged`), and the goal's move stays theirs, but quietly: the lamps don't call them for it.
- **Merging from the card.** A lead's turn, or their goal ready to close, lists their pull requests still open, each with QA's word on it and the board's merge button, and a pull request QA needs the captain on has it too: the captain lands what they've just read without going to the board. Above them is the slice's [system recap](../../docs/goals.md#the-system-recap), what to review them by, whatever the lead or QA said since it went up. Unlike the board's, it asks first only when there's a reason to (QA hasn't passed its head, or a merge check refused it): a press is already the captain deciding, and Ops' [merge check](../../docs/merging.md) still runs before anything merges.
- **Its read of a turn ends as the lead's next turn starts.** A verdict that comes after that, or one on an earlier turn, is dropped (it's still in the ledger), and a turn Ops stopped as it went down isn't read: it carries on after the restart.

Over a goal's pod, the team screen shows it holding the goal's move while it reads the turn, and while its words count down to sending themselves (`moveRules.ts`); a card, an interrupt, words held (or that didn't go once their time came) or a read that took too long it hands to the captain, ahead of QA testing what the lead shipped, since only it knows they're waiting on the captain.

Only a lead's ended turn is the chief of staff's to judge: a raised hand, a question, an error, a goal ready to close and QA's calls go to the captain whatever it thinks. Every verdict is still kept as a `cos-verdict` line, to check against what the captain did (`captain-replies`, `captain-decision`).

## The watch

Comms is the bridge: one goal at the conn, the rest underway on their orders (`stream.ts`, `watchRules.ts`). The user picked it (28 October 2026) from the grammar in `docs/interface-style/mockups/bridge-grammar.html`, after a day of 66 switches between goals with every one of them asking at the same weight. Its principle: a thing sits as high as the attention it asks of the captain right now, and the higher it sits, the sooner it has to leave.

- **The rail** is every shown project's goals, each in its berth, staying where it is: what's waiting on a goal is a count on its berth, not a reordering of the list. Pressing a berth, or its number (1–9), takes the conn of that goal. A berth whose count goes up during a watch is ringed in amber until it ends.
- **At the conn**, the goal is the sheet in the viewer: its head (the lead's name is **Talk**), what came into it since the watch began, its exhibits (`exhibitsOf`: a count in the head, **Exhibits**, or E, opening a panel beside the sheet that lists every one by slice or newest first, a press opening it on the sheet), and what it asks of the captain as cards resting on it: its decision (headed by the chief of staff's reading, answered by `actsOf`: the pick first and marked, the other choices named for what they do, **Discuss**, **Got it**, and the lead's open pull requests folded under More; the same answers on a hail and a muster row), words the chief of staff has ready (Send, or Got it), a slice that landed in Show (Got it), and their preview while one runs. The exhibits were first a strip on the sheet (option D of `docs/interface-style/mockups/exhibits-grammar.html`), each slice's exhibits on one track, since the sheet had shown only the latest exhibit and lost the ones before it. The captain found the strip confusing, and took the slices folded behind into stacks for missing exhibits, so it became a count and a panel they open when they want it (28 October 2026). The other berths dim, their counts still lit. Focus keeps the conn (`conn` in the plugin's state), so it survives a reload and a restart, and the goal's lead going ends the watch. With nobody at the conn the viewer is an empty table.
- **While on watch, only a hail breaks it.** A lead's turn the chief of staff judged an interrupt for what can't be undone or reaches beyond the ship (P5) or for the lead contradicting the captain (P6) is a hail: it toasts and sits over the sheet's corner, put on screen there or told to **stand by** into the muster (`stoodBy`, by its inbox id, let go once it no longer waits). Every other decision of another goal's is a count on its berth and in the muster, without a toast; words the chief of staff has ready are reports. The user picked P5 and P6 alone, and a watch that ends only when they say (28 October 2026). An interrupt from before verdicts named their check is a hail, by P7.
- **The muster** is what waits outside the goal at the conn, ordered by what each unblocks: hails, then someone idle until the captain answers, then the rest, oldest first. The Muster chip (or M) stands down from a watch and brings it up, and with no watch brings it up or puts it away. Each row opens its answers in place; the reports (words ready for a lead, slices that landed in Show) are one row at its foot, heard together with **Got it**.
- **Talk**: the lead's name on the sheet (or T) opens the panel beside the sheet: the lead's words and the captain's, and a box whose words go to the lead on Enter. Esc puts it away.
- **Its width** is capped (`TW.table`), and the panel opens beside the sheet rather than pushing it aside. On a wide screen the sheet ran the full width, decision cards and all, and the panel was too thin to talk in; sliding and scaling the sheet to make room for it made it bob (28 October 2026). The orders line (done looks like, what the lead learned) went too: the captain couldn't open them, and didn't know what they were for.
- **Its look** is the mockup's, value for value: Comms opens in the panel's flight-deck look (`look: 'flight'`), and the bridge is drawn with Tailwind's utilities (`bridge.css`: prefixed `tw:`, without Tailwind's preflight, and !important so the ship's unlayered styles don't win). The user asked for exactly what the mockup has and nothing more (28 October 2026), so the thread, the quick replies and the lead's workspace tabs went: the conversation is the panel's, and what the lead put up is the exhibit's.
- **A decision's answers** were the inbox card's whole kit at first (merges, quick replies, what the lead showed, a reply box, their panel), restyled. The captain found it cluttered and asked for clean buttons and a way to talk it over (28 October 2026), so the card has only the grammar's verbs. **Discuss** slides the panel in with the decision quoted at its top, and the captain's words go to the lead as being about it. Replying in your own words is Discuss, so the reply box went.
- **A decision's brief** is the chief of staff's words to the captain (`briefOf`), each call with its pick and alternatives: the card showed why it routed the turn instead, which says why it's the captain's, not what they're choosing (28 October 2026). Its suggested replies are choices the card's width, each with the words it sends under its name, since a label like "Hold, but switch over" alone doesn't say what it decides; the first is marked as the pick. A decision answered, on its card or in the chat, stays faded and out of reach until the lead's next one takes its place, so it doesn't vanish from under the captain.
- **View in room** (the head's button, or B) tunes the room's goal viewing station to the goal at the conn and takes you there; any other lead's berth has its own while it's hovered. Discuss opens the panel at the lead's latest words, from their start, and the conversation wraps rather than scrolling sideways.
- **Still to build** from the grammar: the exhibit's questions and marks, probes on a decision's contested part, and orders made from each make-it-so.

## The direct line

At the top of Comms' rail, above every project's goals, is the chief of staff itself (**Chief of Staff**, or 0): taking it stands down from any watch and puts its conversation in the viewer's place, with a box to talk in, as the Talk panel looks (`line.ts`, `lineView.ts`, `lineRules.ts`). The user asked for it (29 October 2026) to talk work over with the chief of staff without first picking the goal it belongs to, and decided it has free rein there: when the conversation calls for work, it puts it where it belongs by itself, with no step asking them first.

- **Where work goes:** each message comes to it with the projects it's switched on for, their open goals (each lead's id, the goal's title, how they stand, and what waits on the user), and the goals it started from the line (`crew/line.md`). Work that fits an open goal goes to that goal's lead (`tell_goal`, heard as `[Chief of Staff]`), since an open goal already has the context; only work separate from every open goal starts a new one (`start_goal`, in the project it picks, from `the chief of staff’s direct line`). A question, or an idea the user is still turning over, starts nothing.
- **It says so:** a goal it starts is a notice in the conversation where it happened, "I started *the goal* in *the project* because *why*", with **Close**, its undo: Focus closes the goal as the user would, warning first about work that would be lost. Words it told a lead are a quieter line.
- **It starts a goal once, however often a turn asks for it: a retried turn, or one resumed after a restart.** Each goal it starts is kept on the line by its project and its words, normalised (`goalKey`); asked for the same goal while that one is open, the tool finds it rather than starting another, and tells the session so. A goal the user closed from the line (or that's gone some other way) may be started again. It keeps it as the goal starts, not as the turn ends, so a restart between the two still has it. Words it told a lead are kept the same way, so the same words never go to the same lead twice.
- **Close closes the goal it started, once however often it's pressed.**
- **Each goal it starts waits in the muster until the user hears of it or closes it**, so they learn of it without being on the line: "Chief of Staff started *the goal* in *the project*: *why*", counted on the Muster chip and the HUD's Comms badge like the rest of what waits on them, its title a press to take the goal's conn. **Close** there is the line's Close; **Got it lets the goal run on, and is kept across a restart**, as the goal it started (`startedWaiting`, `lineRules.ts`). It isn't an inbox item: those are Focus's hails, worked out from the agents, where this is the line's own record.
- **It carries on across a restart:** the conversation and its Claude Code session are kept, each turn resuming it, and without a session (a new conversation) it's told the conversation so far. **A turn a restart cut ends failed, saying so, and the line takes the next message.** **New conversation** starts afresh; the goals it started stay kept, so none starts twice.
- **What it can do:** read the ship (`ship_status`), any project's code (`open_project`, Read, Glob, Grep), start a goal and tell a lead. Nothing else: no edits, issues, merges or closes but the user's Close. It's the only place the chief of staff starts work: judging a lead's turn, P3 still holds.

## A thread per goal

Comms is moving to one stream per project, a thread per goal in it: the captain picked that layout (B of three mockups, 25 October 2026) because one project interleaves several goals' conversations, and a thread keeps each one readable while what needs them stays pinned. A goal's thread (`thread.ts`, served at `/thread/:id` by its lead's id) is its conversation in order: what the captain said to the lead, what the lead said as each turn ended, and, as quieter lines, what Ops, QA and the chief of staff told the lead, its pull requests merged, where the chief of staff sent each turn, and a card the user hadn't answered that the lead carried on past (`Close the goal? — set aside: Rhee carried on`).

- **The stream** (`stream.ts`, `streamList.ts`) was what C, the bridge chair and the Comms panel opened, until [the watch](#the-watch) replaced it: the rail took the goals list's place, the sheet the thread's, and the panel the lead's workspace. What follows is how it was; the thread route (`thread.ts`) still serves what the sheet and the panel read. The user picked its layout from the stream lab (26 October 2026, `lab/`, "Your pick"): on the left every shown project's goals in one list, what needs them first and then each project's others under its name, a compact row each (a dot for its state, how long since anything came in), and what waits on them outside any goal's conversation; the list folds to a strip. On the right the goal picked: what's agreed on it up top (done looks like, its pull requests merged), its thread told by the chief of staff (below), quiet lines between messages bundled ("4 updates · merged #689 · QA passed"), a line where what's new since they last looked begins (kept in this browser), and what needs them on that goal asked at the thread's end, the card Comms always showed, answered the same way. At the foot, quick replies to the lead. At the right, the lead's workspace (`workspace.ts`): a tab each for their conversation (their words and the user's alone), what they put on their station's screen, the preview of their pull request when the project is running one, their pull requests with QA's word on each, and what they've been doing (their tools and errors); the box to talk to them is at its foot, since it's who it goes to. It folds to a strip as the goals do. The single list it replaced (`comms.ts`) is retired: the user found the stream better (26 October 2026). What only it showed now ends a goal's thread too: words the chief of staff has ready for the lead (sent with a press, or counted down and held), when nothing there needs the captain, and what of the goal's landed in Show; a slice that landed with its lead gone is a line on the left, heard with **Got it**. Recently decided isn't shown: the inbox API still serves it.
- **The centre is the chief of staff's:** each of the lead's turns gives way to its reading of it, written for the user (`says` on its verdict, or `why` on a verdict from before it wrote one), with ways into the lead's workspace on what it read: their words, the visuals they put up meanwhile, their preview (`cosRows`, `streamList.test.ts`, `stream.test.ts`). The user asked for it because the lead's report followed by the chief of staff's reading of it said everything twice. A turn it hasn't read (it's reading it, or it's off for the project) stays as the lead said it. What it asks of the user on that turn comes as the card under its reading, not in place of it: on a long report a reading can drop the lesser points, which the card and the lead's words keep.
- **Its words are set to read:** a plain sans at 16px, not the ship's mono reading face, because the user found the denser screens hard going and picked the mockups set that way; they tried the mono on the stream (#688) and came back to the sans, title and buttons included, for now.
- **Nothing new is kept:** it's read from the lead's log, transcript and the ledger as it's asked for (`thread.test.ts`).
- **Trying other ways to draw it:** `lab/index.html`, opened straight from disk, draws the stream from sample data split into slots (frame, goals, header, voice, messages, quiet lines, what needs the user, talk box, skin), each drawn as it is today or by one of twenty-eight variations, combined one per slot. Nothing in it reaches Focus: it's for picking a direction before building it into `stream.ts`.
- **The captain's words come from the lead's log, not the transcript:** there, Ops' news waiting for the lead is joined to the captain's next message, so their words would be lost under it; the log keeps each message whole, with who sent it.

## Raising a pull request's lane

A goal's lead puts each of their pull requests in a [lane](../../docs/merging.md#lanes-how-a-goals-leads-slice-merges) with the recap they put on it: Ship merges by itself once QA passes it, Show merges and tells the captain, Ask waits for them. The lead picks it, so a lead that misjudges what the captain would want to see would merge it unseen; the chief of staff is the second pair of eyes, and it may raise the lane, never lower it (`crew/lane.md`, `laneJudge.ts`, `laneRules.ts`, their tests and `chief.test.ts`):

- **It reads each pull request once,** as the project's board refreshes after the lead's recap: the goal, the pull request's title and description, the lead's pick and its recap, by the lane rules and the captain's standing orders, with the model it judges a turn with. Its answer is a `cos-lane` ledger line, kept or raised, with why and its prompt's version, to check against what the captain did with it.
- **Nothing in Ship or Show merges by itself before it has read it** at the commit its recap was put on at (`mergeHeld`): a new recap is read again. Ask is as high as it goes, so it isn't read.
- **One it couldn't read goes in Ask,** since a pull request it hasn't read is the captain's, and the `cos-lane` line says why (`failed`).
- **A raise is Ops'**, through `host.goals.raiseLane`, on that pull request alone: it goes up, with a `lane-raised` line and a comment on it saying why.

## Landed in Show

A slice the lead (or the chief of staff) put in Show merges by itself once QA passes it, like Ship, and then the captain hears of it: a card of its own, off the badge, since nothing waits on them (`landed.ts`; `landed.test.ts`, `captainsInbox.test.ts`). It ends its goal's thread in Comms, or with its lead gone, is a line on the left of it; the bot has it too (the inbox API's `landed`). The captain asked for a card a slice, not a daily digest, so each is heard of as it lands.

- **It's the ledger's:** a `merged` line Ops made (`by: office`) in Show, with its lead, that no `captain-decision` answers yet, so the inbox keeps nothing and a restart loses nothing. A week on, it's let go.
- **Its card** says where it landed, who, when, and why the chief of staff let it go in Show, with a link to the pull request, whose description opens with its [recap](../../docs/goals.md#the-system-recap).
- **Got it**: heard, let go, nothing sent. **Redirect** sends the lead the captain's words about it, as the captain's message. **Revert** asks first, then has the lead revert it as their next slice, shipped in Ask, the captain's words as why. Nothing reverts by itself: Focus has no revert of its own, and the lead knows the work.
- Each is a `captain-decision` (item kind `landed`). Redirect and Revert need the goal still open; a closed goal's is refused, and the card stays for the captain to follow up on GitHub.

## The lead's news it wakes them for

Only the captain starts a goal lead's next turn in the core: Ops' and QA's news for the lead is a note, heard with the next message they get ([goals](../../docs/goals.md)). Where the chief of staff is on, it wakes the lead for the news that needs no captain, as whoever the note is from, and the note goes ahead of its words (`wakeRules.ts`):

- **QA fails their pull request**, in a project that sends fails back (**Send failed PRs back**): back to the lead to fix it and ship again, or tell the captain why not, twice at most (`LEAD_FIX_ROUNDS`). Past that it's the captain's, and Ops' toast says the lead waits on them. A fail on a commit they've shipped past isn't sent back, and isn't the captain's either: QA is reviewing their latest already, so there's no toast. Nor is one Ops sent to a fixer of its own (the note names them): it wakes the lead for that fail only if the fix comes back to them.
- **Their slice lands**: they carry on with what's next if it's within what the captain has agreed (what done looks like for the goal, and the calls they've made), and otherwise say what they'd do and stop for them (waiting for a go-ahead after each slice was the biggest wait on the captain: [where goals' time goes](../flow/README.md)). In the middle of a turn, they carry on where they are.

It never wakes a lead the captain owes an answer: a turn it judged a card or an interrupt, one it never judged, a question, a goal ready to close, an error, read or not; nor one the captain stopped. A turn it would carry on or send back wakes them, read or not, and so does one the captain acknowledged (Got it: they're owed nothing). Waking them would start their next turn and take what waited on the captain from their inbox unanswered, so the note waits for the captain's next message instead. News for a turn it's still reading waits for its verdict: it wakes the lead then if the turn is one it would carry on or send back.

## A cap on rounds without the captain

Its words sending themselves and its wakes each start a lead's next turn without the captain, so a goal could go back and forth for hours unseen. After a few such rounds in a row (`UNATTENDED_ROUNDS`, `roundRules.ts`), as `LEAD_FIX_ROUNDS` caps QA's send-backs, the next turn it would carry on or send back is a card instead: its words for the lead are offered as a reply (**Send its words**), not sent, and since the captain owes the lead an answer, Ops' news waits too. The `cos-verdict` line keeps what it judged.

- **A round** is a lead's turn it judged, but the one the captain started: counted from the ledger, back to the captain's last `captain-decision` on that lead (its own sends don't count), so a restart loses nothing (`roundRules.test.ts`, `chief.test.ts`).
- **Anything the captain decides** starts the count again: a reply, Got it, a suggested reply, sending its words themselves. A message to a lead at work isn't a decision the inbox records, so it doesn't; the cap only comes sooner for it, the side P7 picks.

## The four routes, from its principles

It reasons from seven principles, and the routes follow from them (`crew/judge.md`, read by `judge.ts`):

- **P1.** The captain's attention is the scarcest thing aboard: spend it where their judgement changes what gets built.
- **P2.** Direction is the captain's, craft the lead's: what to build, for whom, what users see, the scope, the next slice and whether it's done are theirs; how it's built, tested and merged is the lead's.
- **P3.** Never decide for the captain: carry on only inside a direction they've set. Anything that starts, widens, narrows, redirects or ends work is theirs, standing orders or not. The one exception is [its direct line](#the-direct-line): there the user has given it the say to start a goal when their conversation calls for one, and it tells them it did. Reading a lead's turn, it never starts, widens or redirects work.
- **P4.** Prepare, don't pass through: a decision reaches them answerable in seconds, with options and a pick.
- **P5.** What can't be undone, or reaches beyond the ship (money, production data, permissions, security, licences, people outside), needs them first and now.
- **P6.** The lead contradicting them, or the goal's direction in doubt, is news they need now.
- **P7.** The costs are lopsided: a turn wrongly handed to them costs a minute, one wrongly kept from them a slice of wrong work. Unsure, hand it to them.

1. **Carry on.** Progress inside the direction already set, the next step stated, or waiting on something that isn't the captain.
2. **A card** for the captain's queue, the lead's pick as the default: a decision of theirs the lead has prepared. Starting a new slice, shipping one, or proposing the goal closes is a card.
3. **Interrupt the captain.** P5 and P6, facts only the captain has, options no one can recommend, anything always the captain's. Spending money is always an interrupt.
4. **Back to the lead**: the lead can find it out or unblock itself, its message isn't ready for the captain (P4), or a standing order covers how to work. Its words never approve, pick or start anything.

**Why principles, and how they scored.** v8 was a list of rules, and lists miss what they don't name. The backtest (`~/.focus/chief-of-staff/backtest`: the 242 held-out lead turns labelled by what the captain's reply showed they needed, and the captain's own blind picks) ran Haiku on v8 and on v9, twice each, on 22 October 2026. Of the 200 turns that needed the captain, v8 kept 44 and 46 from them (10 of them carried on silently both times), and v9 kept 31 and 26 (5 and 6 silently). The price is the other way: of the 42 turns that didn't need them, v8 put 20 and 22 in front of them, v9 24 both times. P7 chooses that trade. On the captain's blind sample both reached the captain where they would have about as often (v8 19 and 19 of 28, v9 21 and 19).

## What it reads a turn against

It runs defence for the captain: it holds a lead to what's agreed before the turn reaches them, so it has to know what's agreed. Before, it read the goal's title and the end of the lead's message, and couldn't tell a step inside what's agreed from overreach. Now it reads the turn against the goal as the captain first put it, what done looks like (the lead's brief), the project's purpose, guardrails and intents, and the captain's latest words to the lead from its log (in its transcript, Ops' news is joined to the captain's next message, so their words would be lost under it), the newest outranking the rest (`context.ts`, `context.test.ts`). It makes its checks in order (`crew/judge.md`): what can't be undone, contradictions, overreach, a step that serves no intent, a claim with no evidence, a question not ready for the captain, a step that's the captain's, and otherwise progress.

**Why, and how it scored.** The defence backtest (`~/.focus/chief-of-staff/stream/defence`, 25 October 2026) ran Sonnet with this context on the 759 lead turns of the fortnight before, and set its routes against what the captain did. On the 478 the live judge (Haiku, title and tail) had judged, it put 51% in front of the captain against 63%, at the same share the captain engaged with (36%), and kept fewer they engaged with from them (12 against 18; 24 more sat in the stream, still visible). Its send-backs were the ones the captain would have made: a lead skipping a confirmation it was asked for, a product call made alone, a guardrail crossed, a message that contradicted itself, and a question the captain had already answered. Where it went wrong was carrying leads on by its own reading of "agreed", so it never does that by itself (above). Sonnet costs about 3 cents a turn with this prompt and context (1.3 in the backtest's leaner one), against Haiku's tenth of a cent: the user's call, for fewer turns routed wrong. Jev, where it's on, still routes by the goal and the turn alone, as it was trained.

**It defends, it doesn't edit.** In its first day live it sent back half the turns it judged, most of them for how a report was put rather than for anything that would cost the user: asking for a screenshot of a change QA had passed and merged, a section on what comes next, a second sign-off on answers they'd already given. Each send-back costs the lead a turn and keeps the user waiting, so it sends one back only for what would cost them if it reached them as it is (a call on a wrong premise, work heading where they didn't agree, a question they can't answer), names the one thing that matters, and counts QA's verdict, a merge or a named check as evidence. Replayed on its 10 live turns and 7 from the backtest (`~/.focus/chief-of-staff/stream/lighter`, 26 October 2026), its send-backs on reports went from 3 to none, while the unproven force-delete, both self-contradicting messages and the question with no pick still went back; two overreaches it had sent back now go to the user as cards.

## How it judges a turn

- **Which turns:** a goal lead's turn as it ends, in a project it's switched on for. It's judged in the background: the turn doesn't wait, and a judgement that fails is logged and dropped. A turn the lead said nothing in isn't judged, nor is one your plan's usage limit stopped: Ops carries those on itself. A turn is judged once however often it ends.
- **Its reading of each turn:** the user asked for one on every turn (26 October 2026), so the stream can be told through it instead of through the lead's long reports. A replay of 10 real turns with it (26 October 2026) found 9 accurate, each ending on what the user had to do or that there was nothing, for about 2 to 3% more a turn; on a long report of several parts it drops the lesser points, so it sits above a card rather than standing in for one. It reads who leads the goal (`THE LEAD`), so it names them.
- **Who judges:** Claude Sonnet, in a read-only session with no tools and a structured answer: the route, the check that decided it (`check`), its asks (`asks`: one per decision on routes 2 and 3, each a one-line headline and its replies; on routes 1 and 4 one, in brief what it would tell the lead), why, its reading of the turn for the captain (`says`: a few plain sentences to them, ending on what they need to do or that there's nothing), and the words it would send (to the lead on routes 1 and 4, to the captain on 2 and 3). Spending money is always an interrupt. The demo's judge is scripted.
- **Route with Jev** (per project, beside Auto-send in the manager's **Projects** tab): TypeSafe's Jev picks the route first, in under a second, by the backtest's questions and its proposed policy ([backtesting Jev](#backtesting-jev)), and the judge writes the words for that route. When Jev can’t answer (no key, an error, a timeout), the judge routes as it does everywhere else, and the verdict says why. It needs a TypeSafe key: `TYPESAFE_API_KEY` where Focus starts, or `typesafe.key` in the plugin's folder, which reaches no other plugin. `pnpm run jev-watch` shows each day's turns and interrupts by who routed them ([scripts](../../docs/scripts.md)).
- **What it writes:** a `cos-verdict` line in the ledger, with the prompt's version and what it cost. The version is a digest of `crew/judge.md`'s words (`judge-823b93a9` is v9's, as it moved there; lines before that say `v9`), so a change to the words is a new version by itself, and the same goes for the lane judge's (`lane-7dcda9b1` was `lane-v1`). Jev's routing, when it routed (`router`: its interrupt probability, its pick, what it cost, the version tagged `+jev-v1`), or why it couldn't (`routerFailed`).

## The captain's standing orders

The judge's prompt has the captain's standing orders in it: what they've wanted in turns like this before, learned from their past replies, and what's always theirs to decide. They're the captain's own preferences, so they're never in the repo: the plugin reads them from `standing-orders.txt` in its own folder (`~/.focus/plugins/chief-of-staff/`, or under `FOCUS_DATA_DIR`), afresh for every turn, so editing the file takes effect at once. Without the file, the judge goes by the four routes' rules alone.

## Without it

Switched off, Focus has no inbox: no Comms, no C key, nothing in the bridge's captain's chair, L off a holodeck opens the first project's goals, and nothing sends itself. The core's own plain word on what waits on the captain takes the place of its badge, its toasts and its Needs you on the phone: a count on the HUD, a toast as an agent comes to need them, and Needs you at the top of the phone, every hail as it is, with nothing held back for a verdict. The captain answers a raised hand, a question or a lead's ended turn from the agent's own panel, as before.

## Backtesting Jev

Offline: is TypeSafe's Jev, a "System One" model that answers bounded questions with a probability per option in a fraction of a second and for a fraction of a cent, as good as Haiku at choosing the route? `pnpm run jev-backtest` asks it about every held-out turn of the backtest (`~/.focus/chief-of-staff/backtest/test.json`) and the captain's blind sample, one request a turn: the route as a choice of the four, and yes or no on spending money, on its being always the captain's, and on its starting a new slice, with the standing orders folded into the criteria they bear on (`jevRoute.ts`). It keeps Jev's raw answers, so `pnpm run jev-sweep` can route them at every interrupt bar for free and score each beside Haiku's and Sonnet's runs, writing one bar's routes for `backtest/score.py` ([scripts](../../docs/scripts.md)). It's pinned to a model (`JEV_BACKTEST_MODEL`) because `jev-latest` lags the newest, and needs `TYPESAFE_API_KEY`; `--dry-run` shows what it would send and roughly what it would cost.

**The labels' three buckets are the routes' four.** The captain labelled turns by what they did with them: bucket 1 they needed nothing, 2 they took the lead's default, 3 they brought something the lead couldn't have had. So bucket 1 is carry on or back to the lead (routes 1 and 4), 2 a card, 3 an interrupt (`LABEL_ROUTES`). `score.py` counts route 4 on a bucket-1 turn as wrong; it did so for Haiku too, so its numbers still compare, and the sweep's `agree` counts it right.

**What the first run argued for, and what it ruled out.** Jev routes, at the bar in `PROPOSED_POLICY`, and never carries a turn on silently: its carry-on was mostly turns the captain had something to add to, so it goes back to the lead, in the captain's log. Haiku would only write the words. Falling back to Haiku when Jev's confidence is low was tried on the same answers and dropped: it only pulled the scores back to Haiku's, since Jev's low-confidence turns weren't where its mistakes were. The bar was picked on the turns it was scored on, so the live judge doesn't go by any of this until it's held up on fresh ones: `pnpm run jev-fresh` picks a sample of turns since, for the captain to label as they labelled the blind sample, and scores Jev on them beside the live judge's own verdicts. It leaves out the chief of staff's own words and turns the captain stopped part-way, neither of which is the captain answering an ended turn. On 30 fresh turns it held (7 of the captain's 18 interrupts caught, none silently missed, where the live judge caught none of its 10), so it routes live where the captain switches [Route with Jev](#how-it-judges-a-turn) on.

## Card replay

`pnpm run card-replay` (`scripts/cardReplay.ts`, its rules in `cardReplay.ts`) replays Focus's history to see how every card the captain was owed ended: the captain answered, its words went, or something else woke the lead first and wiped it ([scripts](../../docs/scripts.md)). It knows its words and the decision that sent them by this plugin's own `SENDER` and `SENT_WORDS`, so they can't drift apart.
