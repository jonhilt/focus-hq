# Threads

A project's loose threads, kept past the goal that noticed them. A goal's lead lists something that needs real attention (a risk, a question, something off the course) under their brief's loose threads, which makes it a contact, and takes it off once it's dealt with. A goal often closes with some still open, and until now nothing read them again: they stayed on a closed goal's chart. This plugin lists every contact a project's leads left open, its closed goals' and its goals under way's, and the ideas for next its goals closed with, in a **Threads** view in the project's goals panel, after Closed, and keeps where the captain decides each of a closed goal's ends. **Sort for me** has a model propose those ends, for the captain to accept.

`FOCUS_PLUGINS=threads`, then switched on per project in its settings.

## Where it lives

- `shared.ts`: the register, in pure functions (`closedThreads`, `afterLine`, `register`).
- `threadsRules.ts`: what deciding a thread, accepting a proposal or spinning a thread off writes, or why it's refused (`decision`, `acceptances`, `toSpinOff`), and Sort for me's threads (`toSort`).
- `sort.ts`: Sort for me's prompt and reading of the answer (`sortPrompt`, `proposalsFrom`), and the demo's stand-in for the model.
- `server.ts`: reads the ledger as Focus starts, and each goal closing, decision and sort as they're written, into state it sends the browser; writes the captain's decisions (`POST /decide`, `POST /accept`), starts the goals they spin threads off as (`POST /spin-off`), and asks the model (`POST /sort`).
- `client.ts`: the goals panel's view (`goalsView`).

## Why it's built this way

- **Read from the ledger, nothing saved of its own.** The ledger keeps every goal whole as it closed (`goal-closed`), contacts and all, and is never rewritten, so the register is the ledger read again: it fills itself with every thread left open since contacts began, and a restart can't lose one. `state.json`'s room log keeps only the latest goals, so it isn't read for this.
- **A goal's last close counts.** A goal reopened and closed again is listed as it closed the last time; one reopened and under way is listed from its lead, as its contacts stand now (`shared.test.ts`).
- **Contacts first, then ideas.** Contacts are what a lead flagged as needing attention, so they're listed first. A goal's ideas for next are the slices it would have done next, and many mean nothing once it's closed, so they're listed after, to be sorted in bulk. An idea is keyed by its words, so a goal reopened and closed again with its list reordered keeps what was decided of each.
- **Every decision is a line in the ledger** (`thread-decided`), so the register stays the ledger read in order, and a restart keeps every decision (`invariants.test.ts`). A closed goal's thread ends as next (with who takes it on: you, if no one's named), parked (until when), dropped (why) or resolved (by what), and Undo takes a decision back. The note is the reason a later look needs: parked and dropped say nothing without one, so they're refused without it. Pressing the same decision again is refused, so the same decision twice writes one line.
- **A goal under way keeps its threads.** Its contacts are its lead's to pursue or drop, so the register shows them with a way to the lead, and refuses a decision on one. A goal reopened and closed again keeps what was decided of the threads it left open again.
- **A thread can become a goal of its own.** Spin off starts a goal with the thread's words and where it came from (the captain can edit them first), at the first free team station or in line for one, as the goal chart's spin-off does for a goal under way. The thread is then taken on (next) as that goal, its line saying which: a thread is spun off once, its goal and its line together (`invariants.test.ts`). A goal that's still waiting in line has no lead yet, so its line keeps only its title.
- **A model proposes; the captain accepts.** Sort for me asks Sonnet, once a press and in the background, where every thread a project has to decide ends, in one call (up to `SORT_LIMIT`, the ones never proposed first: batches of the oldest kept asking about the same ones the record can't settle, and a thread said twice is only seen as such beside the other), from the record: the project's goals under way and closed, what each was for and learned. When the project has intents (the intents plugin's compass), it judges every thread against them: next if it serves one and is worth doing now, parked if not yet, dropped if it serves none, and next for the user whatever it serves if it puts a guardrail at risk, as is any risk (a secret in plain text, one customer's data reaching another); the user's own decisions on the project, and which pull requests merged, are in what it reads, so it follows what they asked for over what it would infer; with a compass to judge by, the Thomas & Co trial gave every one of its 27 threads an end, where the record alone settled about 7. Without intents, it may say a thread is the same as another, and leave out one it can't judge, but not an idea for next nothing has picked up a week after its goal closed (`STALE_DAYS`): that went nowhere, and is proposed dropped unless the record says it still matters. Left to the record alone, the first real press judged 14 of some 200 threads, leaving out the ideas only the user could weigh, so the user now keeps the few they want rather than deciding every one. Each proposal sits on its thread until the captain accepts it (one at a time, or every proposal of an end at once, all the drops say) or decides otherwise. What came back is a ledger line (`thread-proposals`) with its model and cost, and an accepted decision says it was proposed, so how often proposals are taken can be read from the ledger. So a restart keeps every proposal, and forgets a sort still out (`invariants.test.ts`): it proposed nothing, and Sort for me can be pressed again. A sort that fails is a line too, saying why. One sort at a time per project, so the model isn't asked twice over.
- **The captain decides; nothing else does.** Nothing in the plugin decides a thread by itself, a proposal included, so a thread is only ever dropped by the captain's own press, and a spun-off goal closing doesn't resolve its thread: that's the captain's to say.
